3800 Days in Los Angeles

August 17, 2003 - December 22, 2013

I moved to Los Angeles on August 13, 2003 and moved out 3800 days later. In 10 years, 4 months, and 5 days, I learned how to explore, make mistakes, fall in love, find some amazing friendships, live stories and discover some of my favorite places in the world. Los Angeles is a scary, big and beautiful place and it will always be home to me. So jump in, let's go for a ride and I'll give you a tour of my home for the last 3800 days and share some stories.

Photography by Logan Muszynski | Concept, Code + Design by Jake Kahana

My First Apartment

3115 S Orchard Ave #119, 90007 – USC

I moved into Century Apartments my freshman year of college. I lived with Ian Serlin, Asher Rumack, Grant Hoover and Brandon Schor. Originally, I applied to the dorms but got an apartment instead where unlike the dorms, no one hung out in the halls or common areas because when you have a kitchen and bathroom in your apartment, there isn't much need to leave. Here, I had to actively seek out new friends and find people to hang out with. I ended up finding a few girls who would introduce me to parts of LA and the improv community, which would shape my circles of friends.

Start         Orchard House >

The Orchard House >

2666 S Orchard Ave, 90007 – USC

The Orchard house was a defining place for anyone who's lived there or passed through. Everyone who lived there was part of a tight-knit creative community. There were writers like Eddie Quintana, directors like Dave Crabtree, designers like Jacob Reed, filmmakers like Lance Hobbie, actors like Josh Duvendeck, magicians like Mike Jacobson, comedians like Daniel Acker, photographers like Clay Larsen, artists like Lizze Leitzell, costume designers like Sarah Schuessler, singers like Emily Roellke, playwrights like Dan Frey, coders like Nick Daze. And we would always get together and talk about side projects and work. The environment of so many creative people changed how I collaborate, work and think about my free time. Not to mention we used to throw the best parties: sing-alongs around the piano, theme parties (specifically the Narnia party of 2006), barbecues, inventing drinking games and fancy art parties.

My First Apartment         USC >

USC

3911 S Figueroa St Los Angeles, CA 90037 – USC

I came to study animation, but after my first semester of animation classes, I realized I could never be an animator. I filled every semester with as many classes and clubs as I could, majoring in Fine Arts and minoring in film. Along the way I loved songwriting classes, acting, science, philosophy, music, writing and yoga. But probably the best part of going to USC from 2003-2007 was our football team. In 4 years, we had 2 Heismans, 3 losses, 3 national championships and 4 BCS appearances. It was like growing up in Chicago in the 90s. (Oh, I did that too.)

Orchard House         Natural History Museum >

Natural History Museum >
and Rose Garden

900 Exposition Boulevard, Los Angeles, CA 90037 –
Exposition Park

One of my favorite museums in Los Angeles, we used to come here for art classes and spend hours in front of the animal dioramas drawing. I also love coming to see the Space Shuttle Endeavor at the Science Center next door. Several years after college, some friends of mine started an alternative social gathering called SIPAUSR, and my favorite event was a scavenger hunt through the museum. This was also the site of the Dinner En Blanc PopUp Picnic in 2012, a really adventurous and memorable night.

Staples Center

1111 S Figueroa St, Los Angeles, CA – Downtown

It's the most theatrical venue in the country. Every Clippers, Lakers and Kings game I went to was like a whole production.

Chano's         7th/Flower Metro Station >

7th/Flower Metro Station

7th Street / Flower Street, Los Angeles, CA 90017 – Downtown

Since they built the Expo Line from Culver City to Downtown LA, it's changed the city for me. As GQ best puts it, "The first time I ever loved LA was downtown." I love downtown, with the big buildings and density of people and things to do. It's so metropolitan and so unLA. I spent a lot of time downtown because of the rarely appreciated but growing influence of public transportation in LA. Once after a date with Margaret Katcher in August of 2013, we rushed onto train at 2am. It was probably the last one, and were halfway to Long Beach before realizing we were on the wrong train. Let me tell you that being near Watts at 2am is not really my favorite place to be.

Staples Center         Bottega Louie >

Bottega Louie >

700 South Grand Avenue, Los Angeles, CA 90017 – Downtown

If you've never been, go. Get the Portobello fries and a pastry for dessert, but everything here is amazing. It's one of my favorite restaurants in the city. It was described as part of "the best date ever" on March 17, 2012 by Charlotte Myerberg. We got all dressed up, took the train down and went to dinner here first, ordering martinis while we waited and ate an incredible meal.

7th/Flower Metro Station >         7Grand + Mas Malo >

7Grand + Mas Malo

515 West 7th Street #2, Los Angeles, CA 90017 – Downtown

I learned to like the taste of tequila from Mas Malo and appreciate the nuances of whiskey at 7Grand. Finally got to try the secret bar downstairs before I left with Jeni Clark.

Bottega Louie         Cicada Club >

Cicada Club

617 South Olive Street, Los Angeles, CA 90017 – Downtown

The first time I went to the Cicada Club was part of "the best date ever" and we felt like time travelers. Straight out of the 20s, there's a live swing band, two huge dance floors and an ambiance and crowd that felt like I was in my grandparents' wedding. It inspired me to take swing dance lessons and to go back as much as possible.

7Grand + Mas Malo         Perch >

The Perch

448 S Hill St, Los Angeles, CA 90013 – Downtown

Look up and across Pershing Square. That's the Perch. Probably the best rooftop bar in Los Angeles and was the last stop on "the best date ever." Little known fact: the bar is on the 14th floor, but you get off on the 13th and take a second elevator. On that floor is an old upright piano they keep in tune, and when I get drunk and go up there, I'll play a song or two. (hashtag hidden pianos hashtag DTLA).

Cicada Club         The Varnish >

The Varnish at Cole's >

118 East 6th Street, Los Angeles, CA 90017 – Downtown

I don't care if you think they invented the French Dip sandwich, or if you believe it was Philippe's. Cole's is one of the oldest bars in LA, they've got an amazing French dip and garlic fries and it's one of the few LA bars that serve pickleback shots. The roadtrip group: Mike, Brendan, Caroline, Linda and I can attest to all of that when we did a DTLA food tour for Mike's moving to NYC goodbye. The Varnish in the back is a great speakeasy I went to for the first time in December of 2010 with Greg Gertmenian, a long time friend and neighbor in college who got me started keeping a drink log, and whose girlfriend Devon bartended for us that night.

Perch         The Last Bookstore >

The Last Bookstore

453 S Spring St, Los Angeles, CA 90013 – Downtown

I know that this is Santee Alley. But I needed to mention my favorite bookstore in DTLA. I could spend a day and a half in most bookstores, and LA's got some great ones. This one, however, on Spring and 6th takes the cake. Incredible selection, but the secret is upstairs in the old bank vaults and abandoned offices turned bookstacks. There's book art, books organized by color, and some of the best vintage books to laugh at and then buy as a joke for a dollar.

The Varnish         Olvera Street >

Olvera Street

125 Paseo De La Plaza, Los Angeles, CA 90017 – Downtown

I usually bring friends who come to visit LA here, but admittedly know nothing about it. I tend to make up facts about how long it's been here and who runs it and where the money from the shops goes. Basically, there's usually solid live Mexican music, great Mexican food and amazing Mexican wrestling masks for sale. Oh. And a zebra donkey. Can't forget that.

The Last Bookstore         Union Station >

Union Station >

800 N Alameda St, Los Angeles, CA 90012 – Downtown

When I moved out, in December of 2013, I took a train back to Chicago. The Amtrak Southwest Chief took 42 hours across the country and left from Union Station.

Olvera Street         Philippe's >

Philippe's

1001 North Alameda Street, Los Angeles, CA – Downtown

The other contender for original French dip sandwich, this place is for the workers. Sawdust on the floor and a deli counter straight out of the 40s. For that reason, it was my grandparents' favorite restaurant in LA the first time they came to visit in 2004.

Dodger Stadium

1000 Elysian Park Avenue, Los Angeles, CA – Chavez Ravine

I make it to at least a game a year, usually against my Cubbies. More often than not I'm with my season ticket-holding friends Eddie Quintana and Brendan Harper. But as much as they love Los Doyers, the thing we all hate the most about the games are the fans who do the wave, hit beach balls and try and get on the jumbotron more than they watch the game.

Studio Number One         Time Travel Mart >

Time Travel Mart

1714 W. Sunset Boulevard, Los Angeles, CA – Echo Park

826LA and The Time Travel Mart. It doubles as a tutoring and writing center for kids and a storefront, whose profits go to the non-profit. I volunteer here sometimes and it's amazing.

Dodger Stadium         iam8bit >

iam8bit >

2147 Sunset Boulevard, Los Angeles, CA – Echo Park

One of my two favorite private art galleries in Los Angeles (The other one is Gallery1988). They always have great pop-culture art and weirdly awesome books and gifts for sale. The first show I ever saw there, in 2012, was abstract paintings and collages based on cereal boxes.

Time Travel Mart         The Dresden >

The Dresden >

1760 North Vermont Avenue, Los Angeles, CA – Los Feliz

I went here for the first time before I ever saw Swingers. But love it even more since. I came here in back to back weekends once, and the first one I saw Michael Cera on a date sitting in a booth. And the following weekend, Mae Whitman was on a date in the same booth (Arrested Development fans would recall that the two dated on the show). Turns out, Mae Whitman was on a date with Seth MacFarlane. All three of them and me love coming here for the never dull, always cheesy musical stylings of Marty and Elayne.

iam8bit         Fred62 >

Fred62 >

1850 North Vermont Avenue, Los Angeles, CA – Los Feliz

It's not the only 24-hour place in LA, but in college, we probably believed it was. Any late night was spent having milkshakes and fries, late night burgers and sundaes at Fred62's. I always loved the art on the walls but never knew who painted them, until I saw the documentary "Beauty is Embarrassing" and realized it was where Wayne White's showed his first thrift store paintings.

The Dresden         UCB >

UCB Theatre >

5919 Franklin Avenue, Los Angeles, CA – Franklin Village

In college, I almost exclusively hung out with improv people. Actors and directors and musicians and improvisers. So when we heard about a new improv theatre that had free and $5 shows, we were all about it. Only later did we do research and find out who the UCB were. We didn't realize how ahead of the game we were. We were first in line to see some of the biggest names in comedy perform here. From Amy Poehler to Seth Meyers, Robin Williams, Will Arnett, Aziz Ansari, Tim Meadows, Jeff Garlin, Jason Alexander...

Fred62         Magic Castle >

Magic Castle >

7001 Franklin Avenue, Los Angeles, CA– Hollywood

One of the only places in LA with a coat-and-tie dress code and reservations-only policy, the Magic castle is a tough place to get into. But when your best friend happens to be a practicing magician and member, it's no wonder we used to come here a few times a year. I know my way around and even know a few secrets about how some tricks are done. Irma the piano-playing ghost is still a mystery to me though...

UCB         Mashti Malone >

Mashti Malone's
and Next Stage >

1525 N La Brea Ave, Los Angeles, CA– Hollywood

Mashti's has some of the best ice cream in LA with odd flavors like tea and rosewater. We used to come here all the time as an intern at the Jim Henson Company. And above it is the Next Stage theatre which hosts one of my favorite monthly events, A Drinking Game.

The Magic Castle         The Jim Henson Company >

Jim Henson Company

1416 North La Brea Avenue, Los Angeles, CA– Hollywood

As a huge Muppet fan, this was my favorite internship in college. I did design work for the Muppets, Fraggle Rock and some other Henson properties. The studios used to be Charlie Chaplin's and remnants of his movies are all over the lot. On my last day here, they took all of us interns into the creature workshop, opened up a secret closet and we got to play with Pepe the King Prawn and meet the actual Kermit the Frog. It's still my favorite celebrity sighting/meeting in LA.

Mashti Malone's         Amoeba Records >

Amoeba Records >

6400 Sunset Boulevard, Los Angeles, CA– Hollywood

Besides the free live concerts here, where I met Elvis Costello, it's my favorite and least favorite music stop in LA. Favorite because of the huge selection and randomness of what I find and least favorite because I can't step into the building without immediately spending at least $100. I have a rule that whenever I buy anything, I also have to buy an album I've never heard of based solely on the name or cover. I've bought some amazing and amazingly bad music over the years.

The Jim Henson Company         The Arclight >

The Arclight

6360 Sunset Boulevard, Los Angeles, CA– Hollywood

The best theatre-going experience in LA if you like spending $20 on a movie.

Amoeba         M Bar >

M Bar >

1253 Vine Street, Los Angeles, CA– Hollywood

One of my favorite things I did while I was in Los Angeles was be a part of the Classic Rock Singalong. Every two or three months, a group of us got together and rehearsed about 40 classic rock songs and then played a show here at M Bar. We'd project the lyrics and people would come and drink and sing along. I met some great people like Joey, Mark, Mike and Simon and had some really memorable nights playing music and singing til 3am.

The Arclight         Postering >

Busted Late Night Postering

Melrose St and Rossmore Ave– Hollywood

As part of the Roski School of Fine Arts at USC, I met a lot of interesting people and artists. One of my drawing teachers was Robbie Conal, a well-known political artist based in LA. His work is shown in galleries, but mostly he wheat-pastes his posters around the city late at night. In February of 2006, my college roommate Jacob and college girlfriend Jaimey went postering for Robbie. It was on this corner that we got busted by the cops and nearly arrested.

MBar         First Adult Apartment >

First Adult Apartment

1312 S Hudson Ave, Los Angeles, CA 90019– Hancock Park

In my first post-college apartment, I lived with two of my best friends Kirill Baru and Eric Zimmerman. I met Kirill when he pretended to be a USC student and joined our improv troupe, and Eric was his best friend from home. As three boys living as adults for the first time, the place got pretty grimey. We had a piano and a pool table, two used couches and the keg from our housewarming party stayed on the porch for 2 years. All that aside, I loved living with those guys and learned to appreciate their living and work habits.

Postering         El Chato >

El Chato

1013 South La Brea Avenue, Los Angeles, CA– Mid-City

It's not here now, but late at night, El Chato sits on this corner. The best taco truck in all of Los Angeles was a stop many-a-night for me and my friends on our way back from Hollywood.

First Adult Apartment         The El Rey >

El Rey>

5515 Wilshire Boulevard, Los Angeles, CA– Miracle Mile

A great small music venue where I met acts like Fitz and the Tantrums and Ben Kweller. I went to the Ben Kweller show in March of 2013 with a college friend, Mike Prell who told me that I'd have more fun if I uncrossed my arms more. That advice changed my life.

El Chato         LACMA >

LACMA+BCAM >

5905 Wilshire Boulevard, Los Angeles, CA– Miracle Mile

Arguably the best art museum in Los Angeles, I was a member for several years. I would often come and just walk around and look at the exhibits to kill time if I had an hour or two. The first time I ever came was in 2003 for Late Night at the museum. We waited in line for hours to walk through an impressionist exhibit after midnight. Other highlights include two of Jason Reitman's live reads here (Bull Durham and His Girl Friday), Tim Hawkinson, Stanley Kubrick, James Turrell, and Tim Burton.

El Rey         Meetings with Eddie >

Meetings with Eddie

8536 W Pico Boulevard, Los Angeles, CA 90035 Pico/Robertson

Ever since living with Eddie Quintana at The Orchard House, we've collaborated on several other projects including Women Be Trippin, The Boy Who Built a Robot, The Magnificent Charlie Greene, Send in the Clowns, and Comedy Sportz improv workshops. For the last two years we would almost always meet monthly here at Nick's for breakfast to discuss the latest.

LACMA+BCAM         In-N-Out >

In-N-Out >

9245 Venice Boulevard, Culver City, CA 90232 – Culver City

This was the first In-N-Out I ever went to in September 2003. Since then, Doubledouble. Animal style. Well-done fries. Every time.

Meetings With Eddie         MJT >

MJT >

9341 Venice Boulevard, Culver City, CA 90232 – Culver City

Every Summer, it's tradition to take a Ferris Day. I literally call in sick and spend the day driving all over Los Angeles, going to museums, restaurants, bars, events, hikes, whatever. Each year the route and sites are different, and the Sloanes and Camerons change year-to-year too. But the one thing that stays the same is going to the Museum of Jurassic Technology. It's become an annual tradition since going for the first time in 2009 with Sam Klein on the second annual Ferris day.

In-N-Out         Seventy7>

Seventy7 >

3843 Main St, Culver City, CA 90232 – Culver City

Enter the generic sports bar that is Rocco's, go through the door in the back down the hallway to the last door and you'll find Seventy7. A hidden speakeasy bar with a daily password to get in. The first time I went here was in November 2011 on a first date and the last time was in December of 2013 with my writing partner Adam Szagjin, where the live music and burlesque dancer felt a little too speakeasy.

MJT         Lundeen's >

Lundeens

9341 Venice Boulevard, Culver City, CA– Culver City

Shhhh. This is my secret place for buying gifts. Almost every gift I've bought over the last five years has come from here. They have everything from books, awesome cards, weird home stylings, jewelry, clothes, tzachkies, kids toys... and no, they're not paying me to write this. Chances are, though, if I've given you a gift in the last few years, it's been from Lundeen's.

Enter through the door in the back of this barber shop to find one of the coolest bars in LA. I had my birthday there in 2012, celebrated my brother Jordan's moving to LA by taking him to drinks here, gone on several dates here and have gotten too sweaty dancing late into the night here. (Not to mention the barber shop does a great job cutting hair.)

Lundeen's         Barrington >

Barrington >

3130 S Barrington Ave, Los Angeles, CA 90066– West LA

I lived on Barrington for over two years and it was the only street where the leaves changed colors. I used to love driving down in the fall and feeling like I was anywhere but LA. I moved into 3130 in April of 2008 with my girlfriend at the time, Jaimey. We lived together for a year and then yada yada yada I lived there alone for another year. Memorable nights here include my Flavor Tripping birthday party and weekly LOST viewing parties from 2009-2010.

Blind Barber         McCabe's >

McCabe's >

3101 Pico Boulevard, Santa Monica, CA– West LA

This was my go-to music and guitar shop. Guitar strings, harmonicas, tuners, books and even some live shows, I love McCabe's for everything music.

Barrington         Bergamot >

Bergamot Station

2525 Michigan Avenue, Santa Monica, CA– Santa Monica

A not-so-secret group of hidden art galleries that, during college, was my favorite place to look at art. I would come here a few times a semester for a Saturday of art and lunch. The last show I saw there was Chuck Close, my favorite painter, in September of 2013.

McCabe's         PUSH >

PUSH

2210 Broadway, Santa Monica, CA– Santa Monica

In 2006, while I was still interning at Deutsch, I started taking a workshop once a week held at Tool. The PUSH workshop was taught by a talented teacher, writer and mentor Craig Joiner. It's where I learned the creative process and is the basis for everything I know about creativity. I owe a lot of my success to Craig and the talented classmates I still talk to and collaborate with such as Dave Carlson, Eli Morales, Keith Rector, Michelle Constantine, Mike Palese, Sorenne Gottlieb and Logan Muszynski (who photographed these images).

Bergamot Station         Warszawa >

Warszawa >

1414 Lincoln Boulevard, Santa Monica, CA– Santa Monica

The bar in the back of Warszawa always feels like we're hanging out in someone's basement. There's movies projected, fake wood paneling and couches everywhere. We got to know the bartenders Al and Vinny and it became our go-to bar for most birthdays and big events. Bar Mitzvah, Emoji or James Bond themed birthdays, Halloween parties and just a regular Friday night. I was here for New Years of 2012 with Mike, Caroline and Josh. When I got there, they gave me a wristband that said, "You are C, find D for your midnight kiss." With Caroline's help, I did, but I think she kissed me out of obligation.

PUSH         Bay Cities>

Bay Cities Italian Deli

1517 Lincoln Boulevard, Santa Monica, CA– Santa Monica

Without argument the best sandwich place in LA. I was a godmother fan forever, but while I was dating Kejal in 2010, she got me hooked on the eggplant parmesan and Mike opened my eyes to the caprese. Get it on their Italian roll (we call it cloud bread, because it's probably what it's made out of) and you won't have a bad experience. I'll say it again. Best sandwiches in Los Angeles.

TRIP

First Accident         Kookfest >

Kookfest

I learned how to surf the Summer of 2010. Every Saturday, a group of anywhere from 3 to 15 friends would meet at Jay Melvin's house at Ocean Park and 4th and walk down to the beach. We'd surf for hours every week, all of us on giant foam boards. My old writing partner Kevin Butler used to surf and film us and make a video every week. They're still online here.

Zinque

Zinque         The Mission >

The Mission >

1746 Abbot Kinney Boulevard #4 Venice, CA 90291– Venice

We used to edit and finish most of our Samsung and Google commercials at The Mission. I can't tell you how many late nights Jeff Gonick and I sat up rewriting and re-editing our spots. Looking back, I remember more fun than stress, hanging out with Nicole, Angelo, Esther, Lundy, Andy, James and Nadia. Or drinking amazing cocktails made by our editor Joey. Or taking a dance break with Henry and Rel.

My Last Apartment         72andSunny >

72andSunny >

12101 West Bluff Creek Drive, Los Angeles, CA– Playa Vista

I started working here in January of 2011 and for two and a half years, this place, much like PUSH shaped how I think. In back-to-back years, we won Ad Agency of the Year. I worked with some of the smartest, most talented and generous people here who were also great teachers and mentors for me. I made campaigns for Activision, Samsung, Google and worked on countless other projects that would often mean many late nights and weekend work. I pulled my first all-nighter at work here and also fostered my love for teaching, helping to stabilize the experimental 72U program. I quit in May 2013 to pursue learning how to code. This website is the first major project since.

The Mission         Deutsch >

Deutsch >

5454 Beethoven Street, Los Angeles, CA– Playa Vista

I interned at this ad agency starting in June of 2006 while I was still in college. It was such a good fit, that I was hired to freelance during my senior year of college. When I graduated, work was slow and I almost was out of a job. But I volunteered to be an administrative assistant. And for 4 months, put in my time booking travel and doing expenses, while staying late working on briefs and my portfolio. I would eventually get hired and stay for almost 5 years, leaving in December of 2010 after having worked on PlayStation, Volkswagen, HTC and DIRECTV.

Goodbye, Los Angeles

3800 Days Later

I lived in Los Angeles for 3800 days in six different apartments in four neighborhoods. I used every day to meet new friends, see old ones, explore new corners of the city, work my ass off, grow up and have fun wherever I go. More of my favorite places and personal lists can be seen at Los Angelists.

Thanks for visiting the site and making it all the way through. Feel free to contact me with questions or compliments or both. Thanks.
